Abstract

Fox, using the live GitHub inventory for DavidFox998, public repository metadata, selected repository READMEs, and public arXiv guidance. The record shows an independent program of Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization spanning Diophantine equations, arithmetic geometry, analytic number theory, spectral questions, mathematical physics, complexity, and software infrastructure.

The principal evidence is reproducible source: Lean developments, build instructions, CI workflows, theorem-dependency discussions, public commit histories, and repository-level mathematical documentation. This packet distinguishes three things that should not be conflated: machine-checked statements in a stated formal environment; research architectures and conditional bridges; and broad mathematical claims requiring ordinary expert review. It is offered as a concise factual record for arXiv sponsorship or editorial consideration, not as a request that a sponsor or editor certify a major open-problem claim.

Author statement for sponsorship and editorial review

David J. Fox is an independent researcher developing public Lean 4 / Mathlib formalizations in number theory, arithmetic geometry, analysis, and mathematical physics. His work is organized under the name Opera Numerorum: a collection of repositories intended to make definitions, dependencies, computations, and proof obligations open to inspection.

The author submits individual manuscripts on their precise mathematical content. Associated code is supplied as a reproducible formal artifact. Where a repository contains a scaffold, hypothesis, imported theorem, or real-number transport boundary, that status is to be stated explicitly. The presence of Lean code, a green build, or the absence of sorry is not offered as a substitute for mathematical exposition or independent peer review.

Formal-methods evidence and limits

The program’s methodological value lies in its attempt to make a proof obligation inspectable. Across the Lean repositories, the public record commonly refers to source builds, theorem declarations, no-sorry checks, and axiom footprints. The Beal repository is a useful model: it separates import-free cores from Mathlib-facing wrappers, preserves the conventional \(\mathrm{gcd}\) interface, and documents one explicit real-number quotient boundary.

The appropriate scholarly interpretation is narrow: an audited Lean theorem establishes its stated proposition relative to its definitions, imported declarations, and declared axioms. It does not automatically establish that those definitions capture a famous conjecture in its standard full form, or that a network of conditional bridges supplies every unformalized mathematical implication.

Evidence package to accompany any manuscript.
  • a frozen repository release or commit hash;
  • the exact Lean and Mathlib versions;
  • one-command build instructions;
  • a list of the principal theorem names;
  • the imported theorems, assumptions, and axiom footprint;
  • a conventional mathematical proof narrative and literature review;
  • a statement of what remains open.

Editorial abstract for a specific submission

The following abstract is suitable for a manuscript that reports the formal-methods contribution of the program, rather than claiming that every associated open problem has been resolved.

We present a public Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization program for selected structures in arithmetic geometry, analytic number theory, and related mathematical physics. The work is organized as explicit, reproducible theorem developments with versioned source, build instructions, and dependency audits. Its methodology separates import-free foundational cores, public interfaces, standard imported results, finite computational certificates, and remaining formal assumptions. We illustrate the approach through formal developments involving primitive exponential Diophantine equations, modular-curve arithmetic at \(X_0(143)\), spectral and Arakelov constructions, selected BSD data, and auxiliary computational structures. The contribution is a transparent formal research architecture: each theorem is to be read with its exact hypotheses, definitions, and axiom footprint, rather than as a blanket claim of resolution of a named open problem.
