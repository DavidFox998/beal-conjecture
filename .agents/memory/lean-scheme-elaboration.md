---
name: Lean scheme comparison elaboration
description: Large dependent scheme-chart comparison statements can exhaust elaboration before their proofs are checked.
---

For scheme-chart compatibility arguments involving nested pullbacks, quotient rings, and `Proj` basic opens, keep the naturality lemmas abstract and stage them by base-change operation before specializing to a concrete Rees algebra. Do not put every chart ring, graded quotient, open, and chosen isomorphism in one expanded theorem statement.

**Why:** Both a concrete all-pairs Rees restriction statement and an expanded generic affine-chart pullback square stalled during elaboration. Later, even pointwise expansion of a composite graded quotient-chart equivalence stalled before proof diagnostics, while the abstract quotient-ideal transport naturality lemma checked. Explicitly restating a specialized product-chart ring square also left the target grading unresolved; directly specializing the checked generic proof with an inferred result type checked. Combining an explicit polynomial chart equivalence with the whole product square inside one inferred declaration also stalled at weak-head normalization, despite the generic ring-equivalence transport checking separately. Even applying a generic composition theorem to a concrete product square and a quotient-induced chart map timed out; composing the quotient map with the expanded square before rewriting to the chosen map timed out too. A saturated coordinate-chart comparison timed out at weak-head normalization even after its quotient equivalence checked; putting the same comparison pointwise or annotating the quotient equivalence did not resolve it. Smaller affine tensor, affine pullback, and residue-comparison lemmas checked under the same compiler and import closure. Raising heartbeats alone would not clarify where the dependent comparison fails.

**How to apply:** Compare canonical quotient maps, quotient-ideal transports, and both pullback projections in separately named lemmas; only then paste the chosen isomorphisms. Specialize a checked diagram by direct theorem application with its result type inferred, rather than restating a dependent product-chart equality. For a chart comparison that is just cancellation of a final equivalence, first infer the type of the generic cancellation proof; then use `show chosenChartMap = quotientMap from h` inside a declaration with an inferred result type. This can check even when the same equality as an explicit theorem signature stalls. For directed polynomial restriction squares, compose the already-inferred abstract square with the *chosen* polynomial chart map using `congrArg`, keeping its result type inferred. Separately apply `congrArg` to the single-chart equality under the actual product restriction to prove that chosen restriction is quotient-induced. Do not compose the saturated integral quotient equivalence into the entire abstract square and only then rewrite it: that larger type can exhaust normalization. Keep polynomial chart identifications and product-overlap squares in separate declarations with small typed interfaces; a generic transport lemma alone does not make their combined specialization cheap. Even after staging, the checked square may use a composition of quotient maps that Lean does not identify definitionally with the separately chosen composite ring equivalence; establish that comparison in a small lemma before pasting. Avoid expanding an entire quotient-chart equivalence even in a pointwise lemma. If a statement times out before proof diagnostics, factor its diagram into shorter typed morphisms and test the abstract square instead of adding denominator-survival hypotheses or assuming a missing geometry theorem.
