---
name: Smooth curve local parameters
description: Coordinate choice and projective-boundary limits for non-rational generic-fibre local parameters.
---

For a one-dimensional affine curve over a field, neither a prime-chain dimension bound nor a unit partial derivative *as separately formalized statements* makes the maximal ideal at an arbitrary non-rational prime principal. An explicit finite-projection local parameter argument now supplies that bridge for the actual generic `Z = 1` chart under nonzero discriminant. Do not infer all projective stalks or a minimal regular model from the affine result.

**Why:** A monic integral extension of a polynomial PID is not automatically Dedekind. At non-rational closed points, subtracting a field-valued coordinate does not generally define a local parameter. Searches in this Mathlib pin did not turn up a ready-made algebraic-geometry Jacobian-to-regular-local-ring theorem usable for the affine Weierstrass chart.

**How to apply:** Keep the nonzero-discriminant hypothesis explicit when using the generic-chart parameter result. Transfer across an actual-chart comparison before claiming anything about integral-chart stalks; prove the projective overlap and remaining stalks separately before claiming all-stalk regularity, a model, or a conductor.

For a finite-projection proof, select the base coordinate according to the *other* partial: use the `x`-projection only when the `y`-partial is a unit; otherwise, when the `x`-partial is a unit, use the `y`-projection and the negative of the equation as a monic cubic in `x`. **Why:** On the smooth curve `y² = x³ - x` at `(0,0)`, the `x`-partial is a unit and the `y`-partial vanishes, but `x` lies in the square of the local maximal ideal, so the contracted polynomial `x` cannot be a uniformizer. **How to apply:** Use the simple-fibre argument in the appropriate coordinate branch and transport the resulting field quotient to the actual chart stalk; never infer uniformizer status merely from maximal contraction.

In this Mathlib pin, surjective ideal-map/comap transport retains the source ideal's sum with the map's kernel; it does not simplify directly to the source ideal. **Why:** The quotient map has a nonzero kernel, so dropping that term without checking its inclusion in the prime would make the fibre-prime argument unsound. **How to apply:** For quotient maps, establish that the defining ideal lies in the prime before reducing the sum; for ring equivalences, reduce the kernel to zero by injectivity. The polynomial fibre equivalence may also require exposing the underlying quotient presentation before its application lemma rewrites.
