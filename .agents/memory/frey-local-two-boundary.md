---
name: Frey local-at-two boundary
description: Why the shared factor of 16 does not yield a uniform minimal model or conductor exponent.
---

For the displayed integral Frey model with positive coprime U and V, the common factor of 16 in the invariants does not authorize a universal change of variables by scale 2. If the unique even factor among U, V, and U+V has two-adic valuation below 4, the discriminant valuation is below 12; scratch Lean proofs exclude changes with positive two-adic scale valuation leading to another integral Weierstrass model over either the rationals/integers or Q₂/Z₂. At valuation 4 or above, integral scale-2 changes can exist in each parity pattern, but coefficient congruences must be checked separately. Even at valuation 4, a single fixed Q₂ change with scale exactly 2 cannot serve both (U,V)=(1,15) and (16,1): their a₂ coefficients differ by the odd integer 29, whereas a common change yielding two Z₂-integral models would force that difference to be divisible by 4. Separate scale-2 changes for (1,15), (16,1), and (3,16) yield Z₂-integral models with odd discriminants 225, 289, and 3249 respectively; each resulting model is Q₂-minimal because no further integral change can have positive scale valuation. Crucially, unit c₄ does not imply good reduction: the coprime first-step pair (32,1) yields an integral model with b₂=-31, c₄=1057 and discriminant 4356 (valuation 2); the identity is a unit-scale successor. Its reduction is nodal, of multiplicative type I₂ and conductor exponent 1, not I₀ and exponent 0.

**Why:** The discriminant scales by the twelfth power, not the fourth power. The same local discriminant obstruction that handles low valuations proves minimality after each specific threshold change gives an odd discriminant. Existence and integrality still depend on coefficient congruences; parity and high valuation alone do not classify arbitrary high inputs or determine a Néron conductor exponent. For the high pair above, odd b₂ gives a node modulo 2 while the nonunit discriminant rules out good reduction; this refutes a universal I₀ claim from unit c₄.

**How to apply:** Separate odd/odd, even/odd, and odd/even cases, then check coefficient congruences within each. The high-pair result rules out one shared triple, not changes chosen separately for each input; the three individually minimal examples do not give a general rule for high inputs. Distinguish the exact threshold (unit discriminant, good reduction) from above-threshold unit-c₄ models (possibly multiplicative). Treat the general high-valuation classification and all conductor questions as open. Do not turn examples outside a Beal solution surface into an arbitrary-exponent elimination.

**Lean valuation caveat:** This Mathlib version defines `Padic.valuation 0 = 0`. Always assume the discriminant is nonzero before using its valuation zero as a unit-discriminant or good-reduction certificate. **Why:** A singular curve otherwise meets the raw valuation-zero hypothesis. **How to apply:** Carry nonzero discriminant explicitly through any local Tate or conductor statement, including unit-scale successors.

**Geometric/conductor boundary:** An integral nonsingular Weierstrass model and a geometrically unique nodal point are genuine geometric prerequisites, but neither identifies the component cycle of a minimal regular fibre or computes the Néron conductor. **Why:** An elliptic-curve structure over the local ring certifies unit discriminant; the positive-valuation singular Weierstrass cubic still needs resolution, and the conductor needs independent arithmetic justification (for example a proved conductor formula or the relevant Galois representation). Blow-ups of a surface are not Weierstrass coordinate changes: unit-scale changes preserve discriminant valuation, while scale-two changes shift it by twelve, not one. A nodal special fibre can have a regular total surface; its lifted equation constant need only be divisible by 2, not 4. **How to apply:** Keep fibre-count and conductor steps distinct; check the total-space equation and divisibility before dividing a chart numerator or blowing up. First-order membership alone is not a regularity theorem; in the valuation-one branch the separate dimension-and-generators argument supplies regularity. It does not by itself identify an I₁ fibre or a conductor exponent. Do not derive an exponent by defining it to match a label.

**Residue-field splitting boundary:** A characteristic-two nodal cubic can have two distinct *geometric* tangent directions but no nonzero residue-rational tangent direction, even when the discriminant valuation is one and the total surface is regular at the node. **Why:** A nonzero mixed quadratic coefficient makes the cone separable; it does not determine whether its two roots lie in the residue field. For a canonical node with nonzero mixed coefficient, the quadratic coefficient decides split versus nonsplit; neither the valuation-one hypothesis nor the first-order regularity certificate fixes it. The two directions are branches through one nodal point, not automatically two components of an I₁ fibre. **How to apply:** Prove the residue-field splitting condition separately or retain both split and nonsplit cases. Do not label every valuation-one node “split I₁,” and do not derive fibre or conductor classifications from the tangent cone alone.

**Ambient height versus hypersurface dimension:** The pinned Mathlib's finite-variable polynomial Krull-dimension upper bounds are `proof_wanted`. General PIT alone cannot bound arbitrary chains below a prime minimal over several generators; that bound uses an immediate predecessor, radical replacement, and induction. The valuation-one hypersurface needs a separate lower chain, not the ambient chain: the translated equation has constant term twice a unit, so variable-only ideals do not contain it. **Why:** The translated Y-linear coefficient is divisible by 2, not a unit. Setting X to zero instead gives a monic Eisenstein quadratic; its prime ideal can be contracted to an intermediate prime strictly below the ambient centre. A minimal prime over the equation lies strictly below that intermediate prime by PIT, and the chain stays strict in the hypersurface quotient. This proves dimension two without assuming the equation itself is prime or identifying the contraction with (f,X). **How to apply:** Distinguish this chain from the separate closed-point two-generator upper bound. Together they certify regularity in the valuation-one branch; neither result classifies the global fibre or its conductor. The even branch still needs its own local geometry.

**Pinned finite-length caveat:** This pin does not offer the proposed `IsFiniteLength` API. **Why:** The modern dimension-zero-to-Artinian proof uses later semiprimary infrastructure; the local proof instead uses Artinian modules, the residue field, and a nilpotent maximal-ideal filtration. **How to apply:** Do not confuse the valuation-one regularity certificate with the still-unresolved even branch, fibre type, or conductor.
