---
name: Frey local-at-two boundary
description: Why the shared factor of 16 does not yield a uniform minimal model or conductor exponent.
---

For the displayed integral Frey model with positive coprime U and V, the common factor of 16 in the invariants does not authorize a universal change of variables by scale 2. If the unique even factor among U, V, and U+V has two-adic valuation below 4, the discriminant valuation is below 12; scratch Lean proofs exclude changes with positive two-adic scale valuation leading to another integral Weierstrass model over either the rationals/integers or Q₂/Z₂. At valuation 4 or above, integral scale-2 changes can exist in each parity pattern, but coefficient congruences must be checked separately. Even at valuation 4, a single fixed Q₂ change with scale exactly 2 cannot serve both (U,V)=(1,15) and (16,1): their a₂ coefficients differ by the odd integer 29, whereas a common change yielding two Z₂-integral models would force that difference to be divisible by 4. Separate scale-2 changes for (1,15), (16,1), and (3,16) yield Z₂-integral models with odd discriminants 225, 289, and 3249 respectively; each resulting model is Q₂-minimal because no further integral change can have positive scale valuation. Crucially, unit c₄ does not imply good reduction: the coprime first-step pair (32,1) yields an integral model with b₂=-31, c₄=1057 and discriminant 4356 (valuation 2); the identity is a unit-scale successor. Its reduction is nodal, of multiplicative type I₂ and conductor exponent 1, not I₀ and exponent 0.

**Why:** The discriminant scales by the twelfth power, not the fourth power. The same local discriminant obstruction that handles low valuations proves minimality after each specific threshold change gives an odd discriminant. Existence and integrality still depend on coefficient congruences; parity and high valuation alone do not classify arbitrary high inputs or determine a Néron conductor exponent. For the high pair above, odd b₂ gives a node modulo 2 while the nonunit discriminant rules out good reduction; this refutes a universal I₀ claim from unit c₄.

**How to apply:** Separate odd/odd, even/odd, and odd/even cases, then check coefficient congruences within each. The high-pair result rules out one shared triple, not changes chosen separately for each input; the three individually minimal examples do not give a general rule for high inputs. Distinguish the exact threshold (unit discriminant, good reduction) from above-threshold unit-c₄ models (possibly multiplicative). Treat the general high-valuation classification and all conductor questions as open. Do not turn examples outside a Beal solution surface into an arbitrary-exponent elimination.

**Lean valuation caveat:** This Mathlib version defines `Padic.valuation 0 = 0`. Always assume the discriminant is nonzero before using its valuation zero as a unit-discriminant or good-reduction certificate. **Why:** A singular curve otherwise meets the raw valuation-zero hypothesis. **How to apply:** Carry nonzero discriminant explicitly through any local Tate or conductor statement, including unit-scale successors.

**Geometric/conductor boundary:** An integral nonsingular Weierstrass model and a geometrically unique nodal point are genuine geometric prerequisites, but neither identifies the component cycle of a minimal regular fibre or computes the Néron conductor. **Why:** An elliptic-curve structure over the local ring certifies unit discriminant; the positive-valuation singular Weierstrass cubic still needs resolution, and the conductor needs independent arithmetic justification (for example a proved conductor formula or the relevant Galois representation). Blow-ups of a surface are not Weierstrass coordinate changes: unit-scale changes preserve discriminant valuation, while scale-two changes shift it by twelve, not one. A nodal special fibre can have a regular total surface; its lifted equation constant need only be divisible by 2, not 4. **How to apply:** Keep the fibre-count and conductor steps explicit and distinct; check the total-space local equation and divisibility before dividing a chart numerator or blowing up. Membership of the equation in the square of the ambient centre ideal (or failure of membership) is not itself a theorem about regularity of the quotient local ring. Even proving that the induced ideal is a closed point of the hypersurface only identifies where to localize; a regularity criterion for that localization remains essential before claiming singularity or resolution. Do not derive an exponent by defining it to match a label.

**Ambient height versus hypersurface dimension:** The pinned Mathlib's finite-variable polynomial Krull-dimension upper bounds are `proof_wanted`. General PIT alone cannot bound arbitrary chains below a prime minimal over several generators; that bound requires extending a lower prime to an immediate predecessor in the local ring, replacing the other generators with elements inside it using radical containment, and applying the induction hypothesis. The ambient closed point can thereby have proved height and local dimension three without settling the translated hypersurface. **Why:** Heights of named intermediate primes do not add to bound chains avoiding them, and an arbitrary prime quotient need not remain a UFD. The explicit ambient lower chain is not a lower chain in the hypersurface: when the translated equation has constant term twice a unit, variable-only ideals do not contain it. **How to apply:** Keep the ambient height result separate from the hypersurface lower bound. Two generators for the hypersurface's closed-point ideal give an upper bound, not its exact dimension or regularity. Prove the needed hypersurface prime chain and regularity before asserting I₁ or conductor consequences.

**Pinned finite-length caveat:** This pin does not offer the proposed `IsFiniteLength` API. **Why:** The modern dimension-zero-to-Artinian proof uses later semiprimary infrastructure; the local proof instead uses Artinian modules, the residue field, and a nilpotent maximal-ideal filtration. **How to apply:** Do not confuse the established ambient height bound with the unresolved geometry of the surface quotient.
