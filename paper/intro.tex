\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Beal conjecture asserts that if $A^x+B^y=C^z$ with integers
$A,B,C\neq 0$ and exponents $x,y,z>2$, then $A,B,C$ share a prime
factor~\cite{mauldin1997beal}. This draft does not prove the
conjecture. It records a \emph{structural verification} of four
honest Lean~4.12 displays used in the level-$26$ attack on the
signature $(4,4,13)$,
\[
  A^4+B^4=C^{13}.
\]
The associated Frey curve is the displayed Weierstrass model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frey}
  y^2=x(x-A^4)(x+B^4)
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
  [0,\,B^4-A^4,\,0,\,-A^4B^4,\,0].
\end{equation}
The short model $[0,-(A^4+B^4),0,0,0]$ is a different curve and is
not used as $E$.

The strategy is classical~\cite{frey1986,serre1987,ribet1990,mazur1978}:
compute the conductor of~\eqref{eq:frey} by Tate's
algorithm~\cite{tate1975}, exclude a rational $13$-isogeny by Mazur's
theorem on $X_0(13)$, lower the residual level $928\to 32$ in the
style of Ribet, and compare the resulting newform space with the
Mordell--Weil data of $J_0(26)$ via the Cremona labels
\texttt{26a1} and \texttt{26b1}~\cite{cremona,lmfdb}. Each of those
four names is inhabited in Lean only as a \emph{display}: numerals,
Finset cardinalities, and a typeclass upgrade. Parent files keep the
corresponding algebraic statements as uninhabited proposition boundaries.
The integrated release does not erase those boundaries.  Its bridge combines
proved Frey numerics with three named assumptions: absence of a rational
$13$-isogeny, identification of the conductor at $29$ with the N\'eron
conductor, and Ribet lowering from $928$ to $32$.  The conclusion is a
proposition-valued evidence record, not a modular-form object.
